Bodies

How long bodies stay on the ground, how to search the pile, each zombie's pocket odds, loot from uniformed zombies and the stamp in the pocket.

Every zombie you take down leaves a body that can be searched. On the action screen you search each one right there; when the fight ends, the leftover bodies become a pile on the map, which stays on the ground for a while so you can come back and search it.

Searching on the action screen

  • Get within 1.3 m of a body: SEARCH BODY (2.0 s) appears.
  • Stand still until it finishes. It makes 4 m of noise.
  • Once searched, the button becomes BODY (n) with the number of items. A body that's been searched and emptied no longer shows up.
  • The time follows the same rule as looting: divided by looting speed (Burglar 1.5×, sad 10% slower).

The pile on the map

  • All the bodies from one fight fall into a single pile (one loot screen). If there's already a pile of zombie bodies within 40 m, the new ones join it.
  • The name shows how many there are: "Zombie body" or "N zombie bodies".
  • The pile lasts 2 h from the last body that joined it. It also disappears when everything has been searched and emptied.
  • A body searched on the action screen and emptied doesn't join the pile; one searched with leftovers joins with what's left.
  • Fights inside bunkers and in the arena of strongholds don't leave a pile on the map.

With the Loot button (the pile counts from the nearest body, within 35 m), the screen shows all the bodies together:

  • Search all (X s) searches one body after another, without needing to tap again. Each body takes 2.0 s and is quiet (0.03, about 3.6 m).
  • If you stop halfway, the button becomes Search the rest.
  • The items from all searched bodies show up together; Take all takes them all.
NoiseThe screen itself warns you: "Opening makes noise. If a zombie sees you, the fight starts and you leave here."

A common zombie's pocket

Each body is rolled like this:

  1. 45% chance of having cloth rags (the torn clothing).
  2. From the pocket: 35% chance of nothing; otherwise 1 item (70%) or 2 items (30%) from the list below.
  3. If ammo comes up, it's 1 to 4 rounds.
  4. 6% chance of having a stamp (a letter in the pocket).

Pocket list (each with weight 1, except where noted):

rag, bandage, water bottle, crackers, chocolate, lighter, cigarettes (weight 2: appears twice), batteries, kitchen knife, painkiller, box of canned food, flashlight, 9 mm ammo (0.5), empty bottle, anti-anxiety pills, shell (0.5), gauze, medical tape, .38 ammo (0.5), .22 ammo (0.6), combat knife, wrench, knee pads.

Uniformed zombies

Special zombies (police officer, soldier, doctor...) have, on top of the common pocket, their trade loot: a list of items, each with its own chance and quantity. And a 10% chance that the body has the uniform's full outfit (except the Brute, which has no outfit). Uniforms only drop this way: they never show up in place loot.

The full tables, with the chance and quantity of each item, are on each zombie's page. A uniformed zombie that nobody searched on the action screen goes into the pile with its trade loot already in it.

Outfits go to the wardrobe in the radio depot (only there can you wear them). A duplicate outfit becomes stamps: 2 (common), 5 (uncommon), 12 (rare) or 30 (legendary).

Everyone sees their own

The pocket of each zombie taken down on the action screen is rolled per player and per week: two survivors searching the same zombie find different things, and the same zombie always gives you the same result during the week.

In bunkers the zombies come back every time you leave and enter, but each one's body only pays out once a month. The bunker's morgue drawers hold one body's pocket and, 25% of the time, whoever was in there gets up.

Another survivor's body

Whoever dies leaves everything they were carrying in a pile where they fell, with a zombie with their face on top of it. For the first 24 h only the owner sees the pile; after that, anyone who passes nearby. The pile lasts 4 days.
