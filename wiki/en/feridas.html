Wounds

The nine wound types, where each hit lands, how much it bleeds, how long it takes to heal, the steps it needs and how clothing protects you.

Wounds sit on one of the six body parts and each one needs its own treatment steps. A wound only starts healing when all the steps it needs are done and it isn't infected. Open wounds bleed, hurt, can catch a common infection, and some can pass on the zombie infection. You can see everything in the character panel, Health tab, on the Vitruvian figure (each part gets redder the worse the wound is, and shifts toward green if infected) and on the wound cards below it.

The zombie infection chances in the table are the normal ones; the immunity traits change these numbers (see Infection). Healing times are in real time and count from the moment the last step was done; they're multiplied by the healing trait, the First aid level and the base stretcher (see Treatment). A treated wound stays on the body, hurting half as much, until the healing bar fills.

Notes on each type

  • Scratch, laceration and bite: stop bleeding as soon as they're bandaged. Cleaning is only asked for while the wound isn't bandaged: if you bandage first, the game no longer asks for cleaning, but a wound bandaged without cleaning has the highest chance of common infection (see below).
  • Deep cut: bandaging reduces the bleeding but doesn't stop it. Until it's sutured the card shows leaking (needs suture) and you lose 1 HP every 40 s.
  • Shard: the fragment stays inside (shrapnel inside) until you take it out. With the fragment inside the wound hurts more and has an extra chance of common infection. Bandaging stops the blood, but it doesn't replace removing the shard.
  • Hemorrhage: only stops bleeding with a tourniquet or a suture; a bandage isn't enough. The tourniquet can stay on for 2 hours at most: after that the limb without blood takes 1 HP every 120 s until you suture it (the suture releases the tourniquet).
  • Fracture: makes you slow on the action screen (speed ×0.65 without a splint, ×0.85 with a splint) and gives the Fracture moodle.
  • Burn: without ointment, it has a 3% chance per hour of catching a common infection.
  • Bruise: this is the hematoma. It needs nothing and goes away on its own in 12 hours. It doesn't tear clothing and never gets infected.

The arm is the most-bitten spot: a Forearm guard or a Leather jacket stops most of them. Against crawlers, what matters is Combat boots, Boots and Shin guards.

How clothing protects you

Each piece worn has a coverage per body part (how much it covers, e.g.: the leather jacket covers 85% of the torso and 88% of the arms) and a material, which stops each type of attack differently:

MaterialBiteScratchBlowGunshotWear per bite stopped
Fabric10%30%10%0%25%
Denim/canvas35%60%20%0%15%
Leather85%90%35%5%8%
Rigid95%95%70%30%4%
Ballistic95%95%60%80%5%

The chance of a piece stopping an attack is coverage × material resistance × condition, where condition goes from 100% (new) to 50% (torn). Layered pieces on the same part add up: the attack has to get through all of them. Example: jeans on the legs (95% coverage, denim 35% against bites) stop about 33% of bites to the leg.

When clothing stops the attack:

  • Scratch, blow, melee hit or gunshot: no wound and only half the damage ("your clothes held").
  • Bite: becomes a bruise and you take half the damage. It hurts, but it doesn't bleed or get infected.

Stopping an attack wears the clothing: a bite wears it by the table value × coverage; a scratch wears half that, a melee hit 70% and a gunshot 120%. When a wound gets through, the clothing on that part also tears a little (35 wear points per wound, except bruises). At 100% wear the piece is torn and protects half as much until it's sewn. The Tailoring skill reduces all this wear by 5% per level. Each piece's protection is on the item's page and in Tailoring and clothing.

Thick skin and Thin skin

After the clothing comes the trait: with Thick skin, 30% of the wounds that would get through are canceled (half the damage remains). With Thin skin, when clothing stops a scratch, melee hit or gunshot, the same attack is rolled again 30% of the time.

Bleeding

Each bleeding wound takes health at the rate in the table, and several wounds add up. The Bleeding moodle shows how many wounds are bleeding (1, 2 or 3+). While there's bleeding, health doesn't regenerate and stamina recovers 15% slower in a fight. Blood only flows on the map with the session active: with the app closed it doesn't take health, but rest also doesn't give health back to someone who is bleeding.

Dying with bleeding wounds gives the cause "You bled to death.". See Death.

Dirty dressing

Every dressing gets dirty over time:

  • a clean bandage or gauze: gets dirty after 12 h;
  • with Medical tape (used when bandaging, if you have it): gets dirty after 24 h;
  • with Cloth rags: it's dirty from the start.

The card shows dirty dressing and the Change dressing button appears. A dirty dressing doesn't prevent healing, but it adds 4% per hour to the chance of common infection. Changing the dressing resets the clock.

Common infection

Every hour with the session active, every wound that can bleed (except hemorrhage) has a chance of getting infected, adding up:

SituationPer hour
Bandaged without having been cleaned+6%
Dirty dressing (past its time or a rag)+4%
Shard still inside+5%
Open, no dressing (except scratches)+2%
Burn without ointment3%

An infected wound is marked INFECTED, stops healing, hurts +10, keeps health from coming back while you play (while sleeping it comes back normally), takes 15 off max stamina and gives the Infected wound moodle. After 72 h infected it takes 1 HP every 600 s. Only an Antibiotic cures it; details in Infection.
