Footfall uses the real weather in your area. Temperature, rain, fog and thunderstorms come from a weather service based on your rounded position (about 1 km) and are updated every 30 minutes. Without internet, the game keeps the last reading; if it never managed to get one, there are no temperature effects. The Backpack tab shows the current temperature ("Now N °C") and warns when you're missing warm clothes or wearing too many heavy ones.

Feels-like temperature

For cold, the game uses the feels-like temperature:

feels like = temperature − 4 (if it's raining and you're not protected) − 2 (if it's night)

  • Rain: subtracts 4 °C, unless you're wearing a Raincoat or Windbreaker.
  • Night: subtracts 2 °C. Night runs from sunset to sunrise, calculated from your position and date.
  • Wind: doesn't factor in. Real wind changes the ambient sound and, above 30 km/h, wears out the tarp of a pitched tent faster.

For heat, the game uses the real temperature, with no deductions, and only during the day.

What's missing is the cold level (0 to 2). Example: 8 °C at night in the rain gives a feels-like of 2 °C, which calls for 2; in a sweatshirt (1), cold 1 remains. A coat and a beanie together add up to 3, enough for any temperature.

There's no cold:

  • inside the base;
  • outside a session;
  • for 3 h after having a Hot soup.

Rain, fog and storms

Besides the feels-like temperature, the weather changes other things that aren't temperature: rain reduces your vision and muffles your noise, fog greatly reduces vision and thunder muffles footsteps. These effects are covered in Weather and time, Vision and Noise.

Dress for the forecastOn a cold, rainy day, the raincoat alone already cancels the rain's 4 °C. On a hot day, swap the coat for the sweatshirt or the jacket and put on the cap: the coat alone is already load 2.
